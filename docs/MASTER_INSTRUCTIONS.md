- Milky grey image, weak contrast, and insufficient deep shadows.

  around the rail.

  elements.

VISUAL PRESENTATION — INTEGRATE ELEMENTS INTO THE 3D SCENE

Avoid presenting important visual elements as flat overlays, floating
images, or unrelated objects simply placed on top of the footage. When
the story calls for diagrams, labels, objects, comparisons, explanatory
visuals, or other supporting elements, design and stage them as part of
the animated world wherever practical. They should have believable
spatial placement, perspective, depth, lighting, shadows, occlusion,
camera relationship, and interaction with the scene. Make them feel
embedded in the environment and connected to the action—not like
separate graphics pasted over the render.

Choose the integration method that best serves clarity: model the
elements as 3D assets, build them into the environment, animate them as
physical scene elements, or use carefully integrated 2.5D techniques
when appropriate. Do not force every graphic into 3D if doing so harms
readability. The standard is seamless visual integration, not 3D for
its own sake. Keep the screen clean and avoid clutter.
