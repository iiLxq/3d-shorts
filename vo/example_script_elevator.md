# VO ONLY

What actually happens if the cable on your elevator snaps?

First, there's almost never just one cable. Most elevators hang from several steel ropes, and each one is strong enough to hold the car on its own.

But say every single one breaks at the same time.

The car drops. It's picking up speed. And up at the top of the shaft, one little wheel is spinning faster and faster.

That's the governor. When it spins too fast, weights inside fling outward and trip a latch, and it locks onto a second rope.

That rope yanks up on steel wedges under the car and jams them into the guide rails. Stopped.

And it started in 1854, when Elisha Otis stood on a raised platform in front of a crowd and ordered the rope cut. It dropped a few inches and stopped. "All safe, gentlemen. All safe." That one brake is why skyscrapers exist.
